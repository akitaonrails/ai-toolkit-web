ART DIRECTION (follow strictly):
- Clean vector-style infographic illustration with a retro-futuristic control-room feel, like the NERV operations screens in Neon Genesis Evangelion: flat shapes, thin precise linework, a soft neon glow on lines and dots, occasional terminal windows with a simple top bar. Slightly isometric only where physical objects (monitors, desks) are shown. No photorealism, no 3D render look, no people, no faces, no hands, no robots unless asked, no stock-art clichés, no company logos.
- Background: one solid, very dark aubergine purple (#14061D) edge to edge, with at most a faint dot grid. No border, no frame, no vignette, no watermark, no signature.
- Color has meaning. Use ONLY these colors, each for the subject named in the prompt: signal red (#FD3A37), orange (#FF8527), amber (#FFBE3D), teal (#2FD8D0), blue (#48A2FF), violet (#7854EE), rose (#F45FB0). Phosphor green (#52F184) is reserved for status dots that mean something is running or approved, and for arrows that mean the flow moves forward. Never write words next to a status dot unless the prompt gives them. Neutral surfaces are a raised dark purple (#20102B) with muted purple-grey lines (#443152).
- Status dots: small glowing filled circles, like "●" in a terminal widget header. They are the recurring ornament.
- Labels: short, crisp, correctly spelled, in a clean geometric sans-serif (monospace only for commands and file names), off-white (#F4F0F8), horizontal, never overlapping shapes. Use ONLY the label texts given in quotes above, spelled exactly; add no other words, numbers or fake UI text. Where a terminal needs content, draw abstract colored lines, not text.
- Generous negative space, a clear reading order, large shapes that stay legible when the image is shown small on a phone.
